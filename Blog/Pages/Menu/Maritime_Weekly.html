Maritime Regulatory Calendar — Key Dates & Deadlines

IACS
Jul 2024 (in force)
IACS UR E26 & E27 Mandatory

Cyber resilience requirements apply to all newbuilding contracts signed on or after 1 July 2024. All 12 IACS class societies enforce uniformly.

IMO
Jan 2021 (in force)
Cyber Risk in SMS — IMO MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3

Cyber risks must be addressed in the Safety Management System (ISM Code) for all existing vessels. Flag state enforcement via PSC inspections.

EU
Oct 2024 (in force)
NIS2 Directive — Maritime Transport Sector

EU maritime transport operators classified as critical infrastructure. Mandatory incident reporting, risk management, and executive accountability in EU member states.

IMO
2026–2028 (in development)
MASS Regulatory Framework

IMO MASS (Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships) binding instruments under development. Goal: degree 3–4 autonomy framework before 2030.

IMO
2050 target
IMO Net-Zero GHG Emissions

Revised IMO GHG Strategy (2023) targets net-zero by 2050 and 40% intensity reduction by 2030. CII ratings already in effect from January 2023.

USCG
Ongoing
Cyber Incident Reporting — USCG / MTS

Significant cyber incidents affecting vessels or port facilities in U.S. waters must be reported to the National Response Center (NRC) under NVIC 01-20.

Frequently Asked Questions — Maritime Weekly News & Regulation

What topics does Maritime Weekly cover each week?

Each Maritime Weekly edition covers regulatory updates (IMO, IACS, USCG, EU), maritime cybersecurity incidents and vulnerability disclosures, AI and digitalization adoption by shipping companies and yards, green shipping and decarbonization milestones, and significant market events. Primary sources — official circulars, classification society notices, and verified press releases — are referenced in each analysis piece.

How does Maritime Weekly track IMO regulatory developments?

IMO regulatory outputs are monitored through official IMO circular publications (MSC, MEPC, FAL committee), flag state implementation notices, and PSC inspection bulletins. Key upcoming sessions — IMO MSC 109, MEPC 83, and the MASS intersessional working groups — are tracked and analyzed in the week they publish. The IMO website at imo.org ↗ is the primary reference for all IMO instruments.

What is the IMO Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII), and how does it affect ships now?

The CII is a mandatory operational measure under MARPOL Annex VI, in force since 1 January 2023. It rates each vessel's carbon intensity (grams of CO₂ per cargo-tonne-nautical-mile) on a scale from A to E. Ships rated D for three consecutive years or E for one year must submit a corrective action plan to their flag state. CII significantly affects chartering decisions, as charterers increasingly reject D/E-rated vessels or impose voyage-efficiency clauses. Weekly reporting follows each year's CII adjustment factor announcement.

How are maritime cybersecurity incidents reported and tracked?

In the U.S., significant cyber incidents on vessels and port facilities are reported to the USCG National Response Center (NRC) under NVIC 01-20. In the EU, NIS2-covered entities must notify their national CSIRT within 24 hours of a significant incident. Globally, the IMO MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3 framework encourages flag-state reporting, but no internationally binding cyber incident register exists yet. Maritime Weekly tracks disclosed incidents and vulnerability advisories from sources including the BIMCO Cyber Security Portal, ENISA maritime reports, and ICS/IEC 62443 community alerts.

Why is AI adoption in shipping accelerating — and what are the risks?

AI is being applied to route optimization (reducing fuel burn 5–12%), predictive maintenance, cargo planning, port congestion prediction, and — experimentally — autonomous bridge watch. Adoption is accelerating because AI delivers measurable ROI in fuel and port efficiency, and because new vessel data infrastructure (IoT, satellite connectivity, unified data standards) makes training data available at scale. Key risks include model reliability in safety-critical bridge systems, OT security exposure when AI platforms connect shipboard networks to shore, and regulatory uncertainty — IMO and class societies are still developing AI governance frameworks.
