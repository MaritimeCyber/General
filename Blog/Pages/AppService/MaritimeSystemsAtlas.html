How to use the Atlas

The Atlas starts from a ship family reference profile. Two sibling branches open from every family: its member vessel types, and its system categories leading to systems and released components. Every record has its own profile page and stable URL, so you can bookmark or share a single system or component.

Step 1
Pick a ship family
Oil tanker, LNG, container, RO-RO / PCTC, offshore, tug, yacht — 21 families in total.
Step 2
Open a system category
18 categories, from cargo containment and propulsion & steering to electrical power, integrated automation and navigation.
Step 3
Read the applicability mark
Each system shows whether it is standard, common or a variant for that family.
Step 4
Drill into components
Released reference components list type, typical configuration and parent system.

Applicability legend (family reference profile, not a vessel or project determination):

● Standard — normally fitted on a modern newbuilding of this family ○ Common — owner specification, trade or regulation-area dependent △ Variant — follows a design decision, notation or vessel sub-type

Search works across all five record kinds — vessel types, families, categories, systems and components — and returns bounded pages of up to 18 records.

Knowledge aid, not a decision. The Atlas is a reconciled reference projection, not a classification, compliance, cybersecurity or engineering determination. Component coverage is deliberately partial: 222 systems carry released reference components and 92 do not yet, and a zero count means no records in this release rather than “no components exist”. Verify applicability against the vessel, contract, current class rules and supplier evidence before acting on it.

Why a systems atlas belongs in a cyber compliance toolkit

IACS UR E26 starts from an inventory of computer-based systems, and UR E27 asks which of those systems and components are in scope. Before either can be answered for a given vessel, the engineering vocabulary has to be shared: what the systems are, which family normally carries them, and how they decompose into components. The Atlas gives that shared vocabulary as a browsable, citable structure — a starting point for zone-and-conduit design, asset inventories and supplier scoping discussions, complementing the Type Approval Check and the Maritime Cybersecurity Docs library.

Frequently Asked Questions — Maritime Systems Atlas

Is the Maritime Systems Atlas free?

Yes. It is free to browse, needs no account or sign-up, and every family, system and component profile has a stable public URL you can link to.

Where does the data come from?

From an owner-approved workbook, Ship Classification and Systems v2.4.1 / CBS E26, published through a deterministic, validated pipeline. Each release records its source hash, counts and the checks that passed. The current release is PUB-MAR-ATLAS-2026-08-11-003.

Can I download the whole dataset?

No. The Atlas is published for bounded exploration — search returns at most 18 records per page and the tree opens one branch at a time. There is no bulk export. For project-specific systems maps, use the contact link inside the Atlas.

Why do some systems show no components?

Component coverage is partial in this release: 222 of 314 systems have released reference components. An empty list is a release coverage condition, not a statement that the physical system has no parts.

Does a “standard” mark mean my vessel must have that system?

No. Standard, common and variant describe the family reference profile — what a modern newbuilding of that family normally carries. They are not vessel, class, rule or compliance determinations.
