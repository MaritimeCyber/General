How to use the chart

  1. Pick your role — shipowner or manager, shipyard, or system supplier. Each role gets its own sentence.
  2. Fill the blanks — tap an underlined blank and choose: flags, ship types, class societies, trading areas, where your company is established, the markets you supply.
  3. Read the chart — columns run from company and product level through design, construction, commissioning and delivery to operation. Your lane is highlighted; the other lanes show what the other parties owe under IACS UR E26 and E27.
  4. Open a card for the reason it applies, key dates, sources and a verification tag on every jurisdiction line. Hover a deliverable to see who receives it.
  5. Copy link to share the exact result with a colleague, yard or supplier.

What the colours and borders mean

Each card shows the nature of the duty, not a relevance score: required by law (§), condition of class (⚓), required by contract (✎), or a means of evidence (✓). A solid border means it applies, a dashed border means it applies on a condition, and a faded card means indirect effect. Switch on “Show not applicable” to see what does not apply and why — for example, why equipment under the Marine Equipment Directive falls outside the EU Cyber Resilience Act.

Coverage was selected by objective criteria — the largest registries by tonnage, every IACS member, the largest shipbuilding, equipment-exporting and shipowning nations, and all nine port-state control regimes — and every jurisdiction was checked against the same list. “No instrument confirmed” means none was found, not that no duty exists.

Frequently Asked Questions — Maritime Cyber Rule Chart

Is this legal advice?

No. The chart is a reference aid and a prototype. Results are indicative, based on limited input, and some lines rest on secondary sources; each line is tagged “source read”, “secondary” or “unverified”. Confirm against the primary text and take professional advice before acting.

Which rules does it cover?

IMO Resolution MSC.428(98) and MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3/Rev.3, IACS UR E26, E27 and E22 with each society’s implementing rules, flag-state additions, port- and coastal-state duties, EU NIS 2, the Cyber Resilience Act, the Radio Equipment Directive delegated act and the AI Act, national cyber and critical-infrastructure laws, defence contract security regimes, supply-chain screening regimes, tanker and dry-bulk vetting, the BIMCO cyber clause, insurance cyber clauses, and the IEC standards used as evidence.

Why does it ask whether my equipment is under the Marine Equipment Directive?

Because that single answer decides three EU instruments. Article 2(4) of the Cyber Resilience Act excludes equipment within Directive 2014/90/EU, the Radio Equipment Directive excludes marine radio equipment, and the AI Act reaches wheel-marked items only through future MED implementing acts.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. The tool runs entirely in your browser. Your answers are encoded only in the page link, and only if you choose to copy and share it.
