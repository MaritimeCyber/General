IACS UR E27: Cyber Resilience of On-board Systems and Equipment

Captain Paul
Maritime Cybersecurity · IACS UR E26/E27
September 2026
🎯 Lesson Objective

After this lesson you can explain the scope and structure of IACS UR E27, describe the security requirements manufacturers must implement to achieve E27 compliance for their equipment, explain the E27 Type Approval process and what it certifies, understand the relationship between E27 equipment-level compliance and E26 ship-level integration, describe the software update and vulnerability management requirements E27 places on manufacturers, and explain why E27 matters to shipowners even though its requirements are directed at manufacturers.

IACS UR E27 — Cyber Resilience of On-board Systems and Equipment — operates at the equipment level. Where E26 addresses the ship as a whole system, E27 addresses the individual navigational, propulsion, cargo, and safety systems installed on the ship — and it addresses them from the perspective of the manufacturer, not the shipowner. E27 establishes the cyber resilience requirements that manufacturers must build into their equipment to obtain Type Approval from an IACS class society.

Type Approval is the maritime equivalent of product certification: it confirms that a piece of equipment meets specified technical standards and is fit for installation on a classed vessel. E27 adds a cyber resilience dimension to Type Approval — from July 2024, new Type Approval certificates for equipment in scope of E27 require E27 compliance. Equipment without E27-compliant Type Approval cannot be installed on E26-compliant vessels as a new installation.

For shipowners, E27 matters even though its requirements are directed at manufacturers. The E27 compliance of installed equipment determines whether that equipment meets the E26 Protect requirement's supply chain security component. When selecting equipment for a newbuild or a major upgrade, specifying E27-compliant Type Approved equipment is the supply chain security control that E26 requires. Understanding what E27 requires of manufacturers helps shipowners evaluate vendor claims and make informed procurement decisions.

1. E27 Scope and Equipment Categories

1. E27 Scope and Equipment Categories
1
Equipment in Scope

E27 applies to computer-based on-board systems and equipment that are subject to IACS class requirements. This includes: navigation systems (ECDIS, AIS, GPS, radar, autopilot), propulsion and machinery control systems (engine management, thruster control, dynamic positioning), cargo and ballast systems (cargo control, ballast water management), safety systems (fire detection, flooding alarm, gas detection), and communication systems (GMDSS, VSAT terminals). Equipment that is not subject to class requirements (crew personal devices, non-class-related IT equipment) is not in E27 scope, though it may still be addressed in the E26 Identify process.

2
Security Zones and Data Flows

E27 requires manufacturers to define the Security Zones and Data Flows for their equipment: the network interfaces the equipment uses, the data it receives and transmits, the network segments it is designed to operate in, and the external connections it requires. This documentation is part of the Type Approval package and is used by shipowners to correctly integrate E27-compliant equipment into the E26-compliant vessel network architecture. Without this documentation, a shipowner installing equipment cannot accurately document the network architecture required by E26.

3
Product Security Management

E27 requires manufacturers to implement a Product Security Management process that covers the entire product lifecycle: secure development practices (threat modelling during design, security requirements in specifications, penetration testing before release), vulnerability management (monitoring for vulnerabilities in deployed products, responsible disclosure processes, patch release obligations), and end-of-life security (notification to customers when security support ends). This lifecycle requirement transforms the manufacturer's relationship with their product from 'ship and forget' to ongoing security stewardship.

2. Security Requirements for Equipment

2. Security Requirements for Equipment
1
Authentication and Access Control

E27 requires that equipment implements appropriate authentication for all interfaces: default credentials must not be in use (no shared 'admin/admin' factory defaults that remain unchanged), and the equipment must support unique per-user credentials or role-based access control. Remote access interfaces must require authentication and must support the ability to restrict access by IP address or network zone. The authentication requirements must be implementable by the shipowner without requiring manufacturer involvement — the equipment must have accessible administration interfaces that allow credential management.

2
Software Update and Patch Management

E27 requires manufacturers to: provide a software update mechanism that allows security patches to be applied without requiring the entire system to be replaced, notify customers of security-relevant updates within a defined timescale, provide security patches for vulnerabilities with documented severity ratings (CVSS scores), and maintain software update support for the expected service life of the equipment (typically 10-15 years for maritime equipment). This requirement directly addresses the common maritime OT problem of equipment running OS versions 5-10 years out of date because the manufacturer provided no update path.

3
Logging and Monitoring Support

E27 requires equipment to generate security-relevant log events and provide a mechanism to export those logs to an external system. Minimum required events include: authentication successes and failures, configuration changes, software updates, and system errors that may affect security functionality. The log export mechanism must be compatible with standard collection protocols (syslog is the minimum requirement). This requirement enables the E26 Detect functional requirement: without E27-compliant log generation from covered equipment, the maritime SOC has limited visibility into vessel OT system events.

4
Resilience and Fail-Safe Design

E27 requires that equipment maintains its primary safety function under cyber attack conditions. This is the safety-security interaction requirement: if the equipment is under attack (e.g. receiving a flood of malformed network packets), it must continue to perform its safety function (e.g. an ECDIS must continue to display navigation information even if its administrative interface is unavailable). The fail-safe behaviour under attack must be documented and must prioritise the safety function over the security response. This requirement reflects the unique maritime challenge that security controls must never impair safety functionality.

3. Type Approval Process and Shipowner Implications

3. Type Approval Process and Shipowner Implications
1
Type Approval Assessment

Manufacturers seeking E27 Type Approval submit their equipment to assessment by an IACS class society. The assessment includes: review of design documentation (security architecture, threat model, security zone definitions), product security management process review (development security practices, vulnerability management procedures), technical testing (penetration testing of equipment interfaces, authentication testing, log generation verification), and software update mechanism testing. Approved equipment receives a Type Approval Certificate that specifies the E27 requirements met, the software version assessed, and any conditions (e.g. required configuration settings for E27 compliance).

2
Conditions and Limitations of Type Approval

E27 Type Approval is version-specific and condition-specific. An equipment manufacturer who updates their software must reassess if the update includes changes to security-relevant functionality. Conditions on the Type Approval certificate define the configuration required for E27 compliance — a shipowner who does not implement the required configuration (e.g. changing default credentials, enabling logging, restricting remote access to specified IP ranges) cannot claim E27 compliance for that equipment even if it has Type Approval. The conditions must be implemented during installation and verified by the class surveyor.

3
Procurement Implications for Shipowners

Shipowners specifying equipment for newbuilds or major upgrades should: require E27 Type Approval as a procurement condition for all equipment in E27 scope, review the Type Approval certificate and its conditions during specification, include E27 compliance implementation requirements in the installation contract (who implements the required configuration, who verifies it), and plan for lifecycle security — the manufacturer's commitment to software updates and vulnerability notification under E27 should be confirmed before equipment is selected. Class societies publish registers of E27-compliant Type Approved equipment — these registers are the starting point for E27-compliant procurement.

✍️ Author Insight — From the Field
Captain Paul · Maritime Cybersecurity Consultant · IACS UR E26/E27

During E26 CRSI assessments, E27 surfaces as a supply chain documentation challenge. Shipowners understand in principle that their navigation equipment should meet E27, but when asked to produce the Type Approval certificates for installed equipment — the documents that confirm E27 compliance — most cannot. The certificate may have been filed by the manufacturer during installation, the shipyard during build, or the class society during survey, but the shipowner's records do not contain it. The practical implication is a documentation gap that requires correspondence with each equipment manufacturer to retrieve. For vessels built before 2024, E27 was not yet mandatory, so equipment may not have been assessed at all. The E26 compliance programme must therefore include a supply chain documentation exercise: obtain Type Approval documentation for all covered equipment, confirm whether it meets E27, and where it does not, initiate the manufacturer conversation about upgrade path or compensating controls.

Practical first step: For each piece of navigation and OT equipment on your vessel, identify the manufacturer, the software version currently running, and whether the manufacturer has published E27 compliance information for that product. Most major ECDIS, radar, and engine monitoring manufacturers have published E27 compliance roadmaps since 2023 — many are available on manufacturer websites. Where no E27 compliance information exists, contact the manufacturer directly and document their response. This documentation exercise, while unglamorous, directly satisfies the supply chain security component of E26 and provides the input needed for the Cyber Risk Assessment's vendor risk section.

✅ Key Takeaways — Lesson 35
  • IACS UR E27 is an equipment-level standard directing manufacturers to build cyber resilience into on-board systems and equipment as a condition of IACS Type Approval from July 2024.
  • E27 requires manufacturers to define Security Zones and Data Flows for their equipment, enabling shipowners to correctly integrate the equipment into the E26-compliant vessel network architecture.
  • Key E27 manufacturer requirements: no default credentials, per-user authentication, software update mechanisms with vendor-provided security patches, security event logging with exportable logs, and fail-safe operation maintaining safety functions under cyber attack.
  • E27 Type Approval is version-specific and condition-specific — shipowners must implement the conditions specified in the Type Approval certificate to claim E27 compliance for installed equipment.
  • Product Security Management under E27 requires manufacturers to maintain vulnerability monitoring and patch release obligations for the equipment's service life — transforming manufacturer responsibility from 'ship and forget' to ongoing security stewardship.
  • Shipowners should require E27 Type Approval as a procurement condition, review certificate conditions, include E27 implementation in installation contracts, and obtain Type Approval documentation for all covered equipment in their fleet records.
