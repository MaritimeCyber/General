Maritime is changing. Consulting must change. Talent must change.

The maritime industry is entering a transformation unlike anything we have seen before.


For decades, maritime competitiveness was largely defined by:

Engineering Shipbuilding Operational Excellence Cost Scale

But the competitive landscape is changing.

🚢 The modern vessel is becoming a connected cyber-physical system.
🏗️ The modern shipyard is becoming a digital engineering and data-driven manufacturing environment.
🤖 AI is entering design, engineering, operations and decision-making.
🛡️ Cybersecurity is becoming part of ship safety and operational resilience.
⚖️ Regulation is increasingly shaping technology architecture.
📊 And data is becoming a strategic asset.

The boundaries between maritime, engineering, software, AI, cybersecurity and consulting are disappearing.

This creates a fundamental question:

Who will lead the next Maritime Transformation?
PART I
Maritime Is Changing
The industry, the consulting market, and the leader this transformation needs

1. The Maritime Industry Is Becoming a Technology Industry

A ship is no longer simply a physical asset. It increasingly consists of:

Ship Engineering + OT + IT + Connectivity + Cloud + Data + AI + Automation + Cybersecurity + Regulation

This convergence changes the nature of the industry.

🛡️ IACS UR E26/E27
Embedding cyber resilience into the ship lifecycle.
🧭 IMO · Autonomous & Remotely Operated Ships
Pushing maritime toward new operational and regulatory models.
🤖 AI
Moving from experimentation toward practical applications.
🔧 Transformation Agenda
Digital twins, predictive maintenance, autonomous navigation, AI-assisted engineering and intelligent operations are becoming part of the industry's transformation agenda.
The question is no longer
"Will maritime adopt AI?"
The question is
"How will maritime redesign its operating model around AI, data and cyber resilience?"

2. A New Consulting Market Is Emerging

This transformation creates a new consulting opportunity. Traditional consulting could often be represented as:

Traditional Strategy → Technology → Implementation

Maritime Transformation requires something broader:

Maritime Transformation Strategy → Engineering → Data → AI → Cybersecurity → Regulation → Architecture → Implementation → Continuous Assurance

This is no longer a single consulting practice. It is an ecosystem. And this is where the capabilities of global consulting firms become highly relevant.

🧠 McKinsey · BCG · Bain
Bring deep strategy and transformation capabilities.
🏛️ PwC · EY · KPMG · Deloitte
Bring broad capabilities across technology, cyber, risk, data, AI, implementation and assurance.
💻 Technology Companies
Bring platforms and infrastructure.
⚓ Class Societies
Bring technical and regulatory assurance.
🚢 Shipyards & Shipowners
Bring engineering and operational reality.
💡 The opportunity is to connect these capabilities.

PART II
Consulting Must Change. Talent Must Change.
Talent models, organization design, the role of consulting, AI and Practitioner Intelligence

5. The Problem With Yesterday's Talent Model

Can the talent and organizational models that created yesterday's success create tomorrow's transformation?

The answer may not always be yes. Traditional organizations have often been structured around practices:

Strategy Technology Cyber Data Risk Industry

This model worked because clients could often separate their problems into projects. But today's maritime transformation problems increasingly cross these boundaries.

The client does not experience
"This is a Cyber problem."
or
"This is an AI problem."
The client experiences
"We need to transform our ship, shipyard, fleet or organization."
That is one problem.

6. Past Success Must Not Become Tomorrow's Constraint

This does not mean existing high-performing professionals are no longer valuable. Quite the opposite. Their experience is essential.

The challenge is to combine that experience with new capabilities. Existing experts should be connected with:

🤖 AI specialists
🛡️ Cybersecurity engineers
📊 Data scientists
💻 Software engineers
⚓ Maritime practitioners
⚖️ Regulatory experts
🧭 Transformation leaders
The objective is not
Old Talent vs New Talent
It is
Experience + Technology
Depth + Breadth
Specialization + Integration
Human Judgment + AI

7. Organizations Must Change Along With Talent

Recruitment alone will not solve the problem. Organization design must change as well.

Instead of organizing transformation strictly by internal departments, organizations need to increasingly organize around industry problems and outcomes. For Maritime Transformation, that could mean bringing together, from the beginning:

Ship Engineering + AI/Data + Cybersecurity + OT + Regulation + Business Strategy + Implementation
💡 The organizational structure should follow the problem. Not the other way around.

8. Consulting Has a New Responsibility

This is where the role of consulting firms becomes particularly important. Consulting should not simply answer: "What should the client do?" It should help answer:

❓ "What is changing?"
❓ "Why is it changing?"
❓ "What does it mean for this industry?"
❓ "What are the risks?"
❓ "What capabilities are required?"
❓ "How can it actually be implemented?"
❓ "How can the client sustain the transformation after consultants leave?"

The role of consulting is therefore evolving from:

Advisor → Interpreter → Integrator → Transformation Partner → Capability Builder

9. AI Makes This Responsibility Even Greater

AI is changing consulting itself. This means information itself is becoming less scarce.

AI can increasingly produce
Research · Analysis · Reports · Code · Presentations · Summaries · Knowledge retrieval
What becomes more valuable
Judgment · Context · Experience · Systems Thinking · Industry Knowledge · Accountability · Execution

AI can generate an answer. But someone still needs to determine:

Is this answer appropriate for this vessel, this shipyard, this architecture, this regulation and this business?

That is where human expertise becomes more valuable, not less.
