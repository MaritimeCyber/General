Prologue

"Every door you open with excellence becomes the next door someone trusts you to walk through."

I've thought about this often since the MOL project concluded. The work was demanding, the standards were exacting, and the collaboration required genuine trust across multiple stakeholders — shipyard, class, equipment suppliers, and the shipowner. But completing it well didn't just close a chapter. It opened a door I hadn't anticipated.

In December 2025, our team sat across the table from Evergreen, one of the world's leading container shipping companies, headquartered in Taiwan. What followed was one of the most meaningful meetings of my career — not because of what was signed, but because of what it confirmed about where we stand in this industry.

Part 1 — The Foundation
The Value of Growth Built Through the MOL Project

The successful award of the MOL project was a meaningful milestone. But looking back now, the most important outcomes were not in the contract value or the deliverables — they were in the depth of understanding we developed about how global maritime cybersecurity actually works.

✓ The core value that shipowners truly seek

Not just compliance documents — but genuine cyber resilience embedded in the vessel's operational reality. Shipowners want assurance that when a vessel leaves port, its systems are defended, monitored, and recoverable. That understanding changed how we approach every engagement.

✓ The collaboration process among shipyards and equipment suppliers

CRSI for a newbuild vessel is not a one-party task. It requires coordinated alignment between the shipyard's build schedule, each OEM's system documentation, and the shipowner's risk appetite. Navigating this in the MOL context gave us a blueprint for doing it well.

✓ The essential role CRSI must play within the global maritime cybersecurity ecosystem

IACS UR E26/E27 compliance is now a hard requirement for newbuilds contracted from 2024 onward. CRSI sits at the intersection of regulatory obligation and operational protection — and major shipowners are beginning to understand that the quality of this service directly affects their vessels' insurability and port-state inspection outcomes.

Part 2 — The Meeting
Six Months of Preparation — One Meeting That Changed Everything

For this project, my colleagues and I engaged with Evergreen over the past six months through online meetings and written communication. The challenge with early-stage international partnerships is that the real alignment — the kind that only happens face to face — cannot be fully achieved through video calls alone. We knew the in-person meeting was critical.

🎯 Key Achievement

The meeting with Evergreen produced outcomes that exceeded our expectations — a strong signal that our maritime cybersecurity capabilities are ready to compete on the global stage.

During our in-person discussion, we were able to dive deeper into the scope of CRSI for newbuild vessels and the annual services planned after delivery, aligning expectations and exploring future collaboration opportunities.

📋 CRSI Scope for Newbuild Vessels
Build Phase

IACS UR E26/E27 gap analysis, system inventory, cyber risk assessment across all OT/IT systems during newbuild construction

Delivery

Documentation package, class society compliance verification, crew cyber awareness training and handover

Annual

Ongoing annual cyber health checks, vulnerability updates, policy reviews and re-certification support throughout vessel lifecycle

⚓ Captain Paul's Take

"Reputation is the only credential that matters in global maritime — and it is built one vessel at a time."

The maritime industry is smaller than it looks. Word of how you performed on your last project travels faster than any proposal. When I think about how the Evergreen meeting came about — it was not from a cold outreach or a marketing campaign. It was from the quiet accumulation of trust, competence, and the results we delivered with MOL.

If there is one principle I would offer to anyone building a career in maritime cybersecurity, it is this: do the last project so well that the next one finds you. That is what happened here. And I am deeply grateful to everyone who made it possible.
