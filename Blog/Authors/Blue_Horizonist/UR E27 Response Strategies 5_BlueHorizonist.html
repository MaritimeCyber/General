💡 Insight Article 5 IACS UR E27 Marine Equipment Suppliers

Should TA, SoC, and SoF Be Treated as Equivalent Options?

Article 5 of a 12-Part Series on UR E27 Strategies for Marine Equipment Suppliers

※ The product, system, and project examples described in this article are illustrative examples created to explain how UR E27 conformity demonstration routes should be selected and applied.

Blue Horizonist (Lew)
Maritime & Cyber Security Consultant · ISP Consultant

In the previous article, we examined how to break UR E27 requirements down into detailed, independently verifiable requirements and allocate them across product functionality, product Configuration, the manufacturer’s internal procedures, and the vessel-wide security architecture. The resulting Requirement–Design–Evidence Matrix enables equipment manufacturers to distinguish between what the current product already satisfies and what requires further development, Configuration changes, testing, or consultation with relevant stakeholders. The answer is not simply a matter of choosing between three certificate names — it requires understanding what needs to be confirmed and which route best fits the actual supply situation.

At this stage, many manufacturers ask the following question:

“Does our product need TA, or can we address the requirement through an SoC or SoF?”

This question requires caution. TA, SoC, and SoF should not be regarded as three parallel certification routes used consistently by all classification societies. IACS UR E27 defines the Cyber Resilience requirements that onboard systems and equipment must satisfy, but the assessment process and the titles of the documents issued may differ according to each classification society’s Rules and approval scheme.

The decision should therefore not begin by selecting a document called TA, SoC, or SoF. The manufacturer should first determine which of the following situations applies:

  • 1 Type Approval is required for a product with the same Baseline that will be supplied repeatedly across multiple projects.
  • 2 The conformity of an individual product or system Configuration supplied to a specific vessel must be verified on a project-specific basis.
  • 3 It is necessary to confirm, based on the product’s intended purpose and specifications, whether the product is exempt from UR E27 or whether application is non-mandatory.

The manufacturer should then confirm which procedure, document title, and Evidence the relevant classification society requires for that situation.

This article distinguishes among TA, project-specific conformity verification, and confirmation of exemption or non-mandatory application, and explains how to select an appropriate approach for an actual project.

※ The terminology, issuance conditions, and validity of SoCs and SoFs may differ among classification societies. In particular, the SoF described by ClassNK in relation to UR E26/E27 is used to confirm that a product’s intended purpose and specifications meet the conditions for exemption or non-mandatory application. It should therefore not be understood as a general UR E27 conformity certificate or a simplified alternative to TA.

1First Distinguish the ‘Type of Certificate’ from ‘What Needs to Be Confirmed’

The matters that an equipment manufacturer may need to have confirmed can be divided into three broad categories.

① Repeatable Conformity of a Standardized Product

This applies when a product with the same Hardware, Software, Firmware, and principal security functions is intended to be supplied repeatedly to multiple vessels. In this case, Type Approval based on a defined product Baseline may be considered.

② Conformity of a Project-Specific Supply Configuration

This applies when a base product exists, but customer-specific Software, Gateways, Interfaces, or remote access functions are added, causing the actual supply Configuration to differ from one project to another. Individual design review and testing may then be required for the product or system Configuration supplied to the specific project.

Some classification societies may issue a Statement of Compliance or another form of confirmation based on such a review. However, the title “SoC” and its conditions of issuance are not applied consistently by all classification societies.

③ Exemption from UR E27 or Non-Mandatory Application

This applies when it is unclear whether a product falls within the scope of UR E27, or when confirmation is required as to whether its intended purpose and specifications meet the conditions for exemption or non-mandatory application.

ClassNK states that it may issue a Statement of Fact in such cases. This SoF does not confirm that the product satisfies all UR E27 requirements. It confirms that the product’s intended purpose and specifications meet the conditions for exemption or non-mandatory application.

Unless these three situations are distinguished, TA, SoC, and SoF may be incorrectly compared as higher and lower levels of certification.

2TA, SoC, and SoF Do Not Form a Single Hierarchy

TA, SoC, and SoF are sometimes understood as the following hierarchy:

TA → Highest level

SoC → Intermediate level

SoF → Minimum level

In practice, however, they should not be viewed as a vertical hierarchy because each document may address a different subject and purpose.

Category General Meaning What to Confirm for UR E27
TA Type Approval for a defined product Configuration Approved product Baseline, requirements, test scope, and conditions of validity
SoC A document title that may be used by a classification society to confirm conformity with specified Rules or requirements Issuing classification society, assessment target, applicable Rules, project scope, and product scope
ClassNK UR E26/E27 SoF Confirmation that a product qualifies for exemption or non-mandatory application Product purpose and specifications, basis and conditions for exemption or non-mandatory application

Statements such as “We will obtain an SoF instead of TA” or “SoF is a lower level than SoC” may therefore be inaccurate. In particular, ClassNK’s UR E26/E27 SoF is not an alternative certificate that reduces the scope of conformity demonstration. It is a document confirming whether the requirements need to be applied in the first place and should not be compared with TA or project-specific conformity verification on the same basis.

3The First Route: Type Approval for Repeatedly Supplied Products

TA is generally most valuable when the same or a sufficiently similar product will be supplied repeatedly across multiple projects. However, the fact that the product name remains unchanged does not automatically make it suitable for TA. The product Baseline subject to approval must be clearly defined and maintained.

At a minimum, the product Baseline should include:

  • Hardware Model, Part Number, and Revision
  • OS, Firmware, and Application Version
  • Third-Party Software and major components
  • User-account and privilege structure
  • Network Interfaces
  • Ports, Protocols, and Services
  • Security Log functionality
  • Backup and Restore functionality
  • Whether remote access functionality is included
  • Default security Configuration
  • Installation Image and Checksum
  • Related manuals and test procedures

For example, if the same Machinery Monitoring Gateway is supplied to dozens of vessels each year and the only project-specific differences are IP addresses and connected systems, TA may allow the manufacturer to reuse common product functions and Evidence.

Conversely, if the sales proposal specifies Application Version 3.2, production installs Version 3.1, and the service team later applies Patch 3.3 onsite, the approval target itself becomes unclear. In this situation, establishing the product Baseline and Configuration-management system may need to take priority over applying for TA.

A manufacturer preparing for TA should be able to answer the following question:

“How will we ensure that the product tested today and the products manufactured and delivered in the future continue to maintain the same approved Baseline?”

4The Value of TA Comes from Reusing Evidence, Not Merely Holding a Certificate

The economic value of TA depends less on the certificate itself than on whether the design and test Evidence obtained during approval can be reused in subsequent projects.

If the product Baseline is maintained, the following materials may be reusable across multiple projects:

  • Product architecture and functional description
  • Hardware and Software configuration list
  • Internal product Interface specification
  • User-role and privilege Matrix
  • List of Ports, Protocols, and Services
  • Security Event list
  • Secure Configuration and Hardening Guide
  • Software Update and integrity-verification method
  • Product-level Backup and Restore procedure
  • Vulnerability-management and customer-notification procedures
  • Product security-function test procedures and results

The following materials, however, are likely to require project-specific preparation or verification:

  • Vessel-specific Network Diagram
  • Actual IP addresses and connected systems
  • Firewall Rules configured by the shipyard
  • Integration with the vessel-wide Remote Access Gateway
  • Integration testing with the central Log Server and NTP Server
  • Testing of customer-specific Applications
  • Vessel-wide Backup and recovery plan
  • Shipowner’s operational procedures

TA therefore does not eliminate every project-specific review or integration test. It reduces the need to repeat verification of common product elements within the approved scope, but it does not automatically demonstrate the product’s actual integration with the vessel-wide security architecture.

5The Second Route: Project-Specific Conformity Verification of the Actual Supply Configuration

Not every product can be defined through one stable Baseline. Even when the same basic Platform is used, the actual supply Configuration of a package system may differ substantially from vessel to vessel.

For example, the following elements of a Cargo Control System may vary by project:

  • Quantity and Model of PLCs and Remote I/O
  • Tank and Valve Configuration
  • HMI screens and operational Logic
  • Server and Workstation Configuration
  • Third-Party Gateways and Switches
  • Interfaces with upper-level systems
  • Remote Access method
  • Security Log integration
  • Backup scope and recovery method

Although the product name remains the same, such a system may undergo significant design changes in every project. If it is difficult to include the complete system within one fixed TA scope, verifying conformity against the project-specific supply Configuration for a particular vessel may be more realistic.

Project-specific verification does not reduce the amount of Evidence that must be prepared. The following matters must still be clearly defined and verified:

  • Hardware and Software actually supplied
  • UR E27 requirements applicable to the project
  • Product functionality and product Configuration
  • Dependencies on the shipyard’s integrated security architecture
  • Responsible party for each requirement
  • Design documentation and Configuration Records
  • Test procedures and test results
  • Unmet requirements and compensating controls
  • Consistency between the delivered and tested Configurations

The key distinction between TA and project-specific verification is not whether Evidence is required. It is whether that Evidence belongs to a standardized product repeatedly supplied to multiple projects or to the specific supply Configuration of one project.

6SoC Should Be Used Only After Confirming the Classification Society’s Issuance Scheme

Statement of Compliance is generally used as the title of a document confirming conformity with specified Rules or requirements. UR E27, however, does not establish SoC as a universal alternative applied consistently by all classification societies.

An equipment manufacturer should therefore not decide in advance that “we will obtain an SoC because we do not have TA.” The following matters should first be confirmed with the classification society responsible for the project:

  • Does the classification society issue an SoC for products or systems subject to UR E27?
  • Is the SoC issued for a standardized product, an individual product, or a project-specific system?
  • Which UR E27 requirements are assessed?
  • Does the assessment include design review, product testing, or both?
  • Does it also examine the manufacturing process or quality-management system?
  • Is the SoC valid only for a particular vessel or project?
  • Can it be reused in subsequent projects?
  • Is reassessment required if the product Version or Configuration changes?
  • Will the shipyard and classification society accept it as the required Evidence of conformity?

Even where the same SoC title is used, differences in Scope and conditions can result in different levels of applicability. Any explanation of an SoC should therefore identify the issuing classification society, assessed product, applicable Rules, assessment scope, and limitations.

7The Third Route: Confirmation of Exemption or Non-Mandatory Application

Not every Computer-Based System is assessed in the same manner under UR E27. Depending on the product’s intended purpose, specifications, and relationship with vessel functions, it may be necessary to assess whether exemption or non-mandatory application is appropriate.

The important point is that an equipment manufacturer should not independently declare, “This product is not important and is therefore excluded.” Its functions, connections, and potential impact of compromise should first be analyzed, followed by consultation with the System Integrator, shipyard, and classification society.

ClassNK states that an SoF relating to UR E26/E27 may be issued in the following two situations:

  • To confirm that the intended purpose and specifications of a Computer-Based System qualify for exemption from UR E26/E27
  • To confirm that mandatory application of UR E26/E27 is not required for a Computer-Based System with the intended purpose and specifications concerned

ClassNK also states that issuance of such an SoF is not mandatory. ClassNK’s SoF should therefore be understood not as a certificate confirming compliance with every UR E27 requirement, but as a means of formally documenting a determination regarding applicability.

8What Must Be Explained When Applying for a ClassNK SoF?

Submitting only the product name will not be sufficient to confirm exemption or non-mandatory application. At a minimum, the following information should be prepared so that the classification society can assess the product’s intended purpose and specifications:

  • Product name, Model, and Version
  • Intended onboard use
  • Functions performed or supported by the product
  • Connected vessel systems
  • Network and other Interfaces
  • Communication direction and information exchanged
  • Whether the product executes control commands
  • Vessel functions affected by a product failure
  • Possibility of incorrect information or commands
  • Possibility of compromise spreading to other systems
  • Maintenance and Software Update paths
  • Technical rationale for exemption or non-mandatory application

Consider, for example, a standalone Computer used to view crew training materials. If it is not connected to the vessel’s operational, control, or monitoring functions, does not connect to the business or OT Network, and does not exchange data or Software with essential systems during maintenance, these conditions may provide a basis for considering non-mandatory application.

Conversely, even if a performance-analysis Computer does not issue control commands directly, it may be difficult to exclude merely because it is used only for Monitoring if it collects operational data from the Automation Network and transmits it to a shore-based Cloud. The connection paths, potential data manipulation, and possibility of compromise propagation would require further review.

9Exemption Is Different from Non-Compliance

The absence of required security functions is not a valid reason to claim exemption.

Consider a Legacy Controller that does not provide:

  • Individual user accounts
  • Security Logs
  • Software integrity verification
  • Restrictions following repeated failed login attempts

The absence of these functions does not demonstrate that the product is outside the scope of UR E27. If the Controller performs an important vessel function, the following responses may be required:

  • Improve product functionality or Configuration where possible
  • Combine the product with external security measures
  • Apply compensating controls
  • Conduct a risk analysis
  • Perform project-specific conformity verification
  • Agree on responsibilities with the shipyard and classification society

The following three statuses must be clearly distinguished:

Status Meaning Example
Exemption or non-mandatory application The product is not subject to mandatory UR E27 application because of its intended purpose and specifications Standalone auxiliary equipment separated from essential vessel functions
Dependency on external security measures The requirement applies but is satisfied by combining the product with external security architecture Restricting product access through a vessel Zone Firewall and common Gateway
Non-compliance or need for compensating controls The requirement applies, but the product lacks the necessary function Legacy Controller that does not support individual accounts

Confusing exemption with missing product functionality can result in classification Comments, additional development, and retesting later in the project.

10The Three Response Routes Can Be Compared as Follows

SoC and SoF are not presented here as two independent certification routes because the assessment purpose and applicable situation are more important than the document title.

11When Should TA Be Considered First?

TA may be considered first when most of the following conditions are met:

  • The same product is expected to be supplied repeatedly to multiple vessels
  • The Hardware and Software product Baseline is stable
  • Project-specific changes are limited
  • Principal security functions are implemented within the product
  • Development, testing, and Configuration-management procedures are established
  • A sufficiently mature Requirement–Design–Evidence Matrix is available
  • Product-level security testing can be performed repeatedly
  • Vulnerability and security Update-management processes are in place
  • A responsible organization is assigned to maintain the approval and report product changes
  • Expected future projects justify the cost of obtaining and maintaining TA

TA is not completed simply by passing a test and receiving a certificate. The manufacturer must continue to maintain the approved product Baseline, assess the impact of changes on the approved scope, and notify the classification society or perform additional verification where required.

TA therefore depends not only on product maturity but also on the manufacturer’s Configuration- and change-management capabilities.

12When Is Project-Specific Verification More Realistic?

Project-specific conformity verification may be appropriate when:

  • The Hardware and Software combination differs substantially between projects
  • Customer-specific Logic or Applications are included
  • Gateways and Third-Party components vary by project
  • Integration with the vessel-wide security architecture is as important as the product functionality itself
  • Repeated supply of the same Configuration is uncertain
  • It is difficult to define the complete system under one fixed TA scope
  • The product Baseline and test target can nevertheless be clearly finalized for the particular project
  • The applicable classification society recognizes a project-specific review and approval process

Schedule management remains important even when project-specific verification is selected. If preparation begins only during design approval after contract award, product Gaps may be identified too late. The review scope, required Evidence, and classification-witnessed tests should be confirmed during the quotation stage or early in the contract period.

13Common Product Elements and Project-Specific Changes Can Be Addressed Separately

In practice, it may be difficult to address an entire package system exclusively through either TA or project-specific verification. In such cases, the manufacturer may distinguish between common product elements and project-specific changes.

Consider a Cargo Control System with the following structure.

Elements Maintained Across Multiple Projects

  • Standard Controller
  • Common HMI Platform
  • User authentication and role-based privilege functionality
  • Security Log functionality
  • Software Update functionality
  • Product-level Backup and Restore
  • Standard Hardening requirements

Elements That Change by Project

  • PLC Logic
  • Tank and Valve Configuration
  • HMI screens
  • Number of Servers and Workstations
  • Interfaces with upper-level systems
  • Remote Access integration
  • Firewall Rules
  • Central Log and NTP integration

The common elements may be considered for inclusion within the TA scope, while the project-specific elements can be confirmed through vessel-specific design review and integration testing. The Requirement–Design–Evidence Matrix should then distinguish whether each requirement is demonstrated by TA, requires additional project-specific verification, or is confirmed through the shipyard’s vessel-wide security architecture.

The purpose of this approach is not to divide the certification scope artificially. It is to distinguish realistically between repeatable product elements and elements that vary from vessel to vessel.

14The Three Routes Should Also Be Distinguished When Comparing Costs

TA can require significant initial investment in design-document preparation, product testing, assessment, and the establishment of Configuration-management practices. If the same product is supplied to multiple projects, however, repeated use of the Evidence can reduce long-term compliance costs.

Project-specific verification may appear less burdensome initially, but repeated design reviews and testing for each vessel can increase cumulative costs and schedule pressure.

Confirmation of exemption or non-mandatory application can prevent unnecessary conformity verification where the product genuinely meets the relevant conditions. It should not, however, be pursued merely because the product lacks required functions. An incorrect assessment may lead to further review and additional development later in the project.

The following costs should be considered together:

  • Product Gap analysis and remediation
  • Preparation of design documents and test Evidence
  • Classification review and witnessed testing
  • TA maintenance and renewal
  • Additional assessment following product changes
  • Project-specific document revisions and retesting
  • Internal Configuration and vulnerability management
  • Responses to shipyard inquiries and classification Comments
  • Savings achieved by reusing Evidence in subsequent projects

The assessment should not be based only on the fees paid to the classification society. It should compare the total cost, including internal development and quality personnel, documentation, schedule delays, and repeated testing.

15Present the Assessment Target—not Merely a Document Title—to the Classification Society and Shipyard

Simply asking the classification society or shipyard, “Do we need TA or SoC?” may not produce a sufficiently clear answer. The manufacturer should first present:

  • The exact scope of the product or system to be supplied
  • The Hardware and Software product Baseline
  • Differences between the standard product and project supply Configuration
  • Existing Type Approval and its approved scope
  • Applicable UR E27 requirements
  • Product functionality and Evidence Gaps
  • Dependencies on the shipyard’s integrated security architecture
  • Planned test scope and schedule
  • Future plans to supply the same product repeatedly

The following matters should then be confirmed:

  • 1 To what extent can the existing TA be accepted?
  • 2 Which parts of the actual project supply Configuration require additional review?
  • 3 Which project-specific verification procedure and document does the classification society recognize?
  • 4 How should product-level testing and vessel integration testing be divided?
  • 5 Which tests require classification witnessing?
  • 6 What procedure applies when assessing the impact of a product change on the existing approval?
  • 7 Under which criteria and procedure is exemption or non-mandatory application determined?
  • 8 Which certificates and Evidence must ultimately be submitted to the shipyard?

The product and project conditions must be presented specifically before the classification society can advise on the appropriate approval or confirmation method.

16Evidence Obtained Through Project-Specific Verification Can Be Developed into TA

It may be difficult for a manufacturer whose product Baseline and Evidence are not yet sufficiently mature to pursue TA from the outset. In this case, Evidence obtained during the initial project can be converted into common product assets while TA is prepared in stages.

Stage 1: Verify the Initial Project Supply Configuration

  • Finalize the actual supply Configuration
  • Prepare the Requirement–Design–Evidence Matrix
  • Address product Gaps
  • Perform project-specific design reviews and tests
  • Confirm conformity using the method required by the classification society and shipyard

Stage 2: Standardize Common Product Elements

  • Identify product Configurations repeated across multiple projects
  • Establish common security functions and Configuration requirements
  • Separate project-specific changes
  • Standardize test procedures and Evidence Templates
  • Strengthen the product Baseline and Configuration-management system

Stage 3: Pursue Type Approval

  • Define the product scope that can be supplied repeatedly
  • Consolidate common design documents and test results
  • Strengthen development, change, and vulnerability-management procedures
  • Agree on the TA Scope with the classification society
  • Pursue TA for the standardized product
  • Manage project-specific changes through separate verification

This approach does not mean that project-specific verification is a lower stage than TA. It means converting the repeatable portions of design and test Evidence accumulated through projects into standardized product assets and expanding to TA when the product and its management system have become sufficiently stable.

17Conclusion: What Is Being Confirmed Matters More Than the Document Title

In responding to UR E27, it is not appropriate to place TA, SoC, and SoF side by side as equivalent certificates and simply choose one. The subject and purpose of the required confirmation must be identified first.

  • Is the objective to confirm common conformity for a repeatedly supplied product?
  • Is the objective to verify the conformity of a specific project supply Configuration?
  • Is the objective to confirm whether the product is exempt from UR E27 or subject to non-mandatory application?

Type Approval may be effective for a stable product Baseline supplied repeatedly. Where project-specific changes are extensive, the actual supply Configuration may require individual conformity verification, and the title of the resulting document may vary according to the classification society’s scheme. Where the intended purpose and specifications meet the conditions for exemption or non-mandatory application, ClassNK may issue an SoF.

Whichever route is selected, the following matters must be clear:

  • Which product and Version are covered?
  • Which Hardware and Software are included?
  • What is subject to conformity verification?
  • Which requirements and tests apply?
  • Where are responsibilities divided between the product and the vessel-wide security architecture?
  • What is the basis for exemption or non-mandatory application?
  • If the product changes, can the existing approval or confirmation remain valid?
  • What Evidence supports the determination?

A sound response strategy is not about selecting the certificate that appears most authoritative. It is about defining the appropriate assessment target based on the product’s supply model and project conditions, and having the result confirmed through a procedure and document recognized by the relevant classification society.

The key question that an equipment manufacturer should therefore present to the classification society and shipyard is:

“For this product, what can be approved on a repeatable basis, what requires additional project-specific verification, and what requires a separate determination of applicability?”

Only when this question can be answered clearly do certificates and confirmation documents become Engineering Evidence that reduces actual project uncertainty, rather than merely serving as sales materials.
