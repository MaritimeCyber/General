The Shift in Market Keywords and the Role of AI

How a piece of advice in 2014 — and two defining AI moments — changed the way I plan, propose, and pursue what matters

💡 Life Story IoT Era (2014) AI Transition Market Keywords

"The vision seems promising, but the timing isn't right yet." — This line was written about seven years ago, and the keyword that fits the blank is IoT, not AI. Looking back at how that realization shaped my thinking about market trends, partnerships, and timing — and how the world's language eventually shifted to AI — this is the story of what I learned along the way.

In 2014, like most professionals at the time, I was working on a research project with the goal of securing a market-leading solution based on IoT — the big trend following technologies like USN, Embedded Systems, and M2M.

In my early 30s, driven by youthful ambition, I was desperate to secure manpower, technology, and funding — which were relatively lacking compared to competitors. I relentlessly pushed for consortiums with global companies and national projects (both leading and participating), convincing stakeholders from all directions.

During that time, I sought advice from professionals who had been in the ICT industry for a long time, asking for insights from various perspectives.

Ⅱ. The Advice That Shifted My Thinking

💬 Key Takeaway from Senior Advisors
"The most important thing in achieving what you want to do is choosing the right keywords for communication — and these trends evolve over time."
🔍 How I Interpreted This
"Read the market trends in advance, prepare accordingly, secure help from stakeholders, and complete the work before the next big keyword hits the market."

I was already aware that the names of emerging technologies change every year through Gartner's Hype Cycle. However, I was surprised to realize that these changes directly impact my ability to secure partnerships and attract investment for the projects I was working on.

Ⅲ. 'Connection' — The Keyword That Opened a Door

Perhaps thanks to that advice, my proposal centered around the keyword 'connection' led to a joint research and development agreement with a major telecommunications company in Korea. This opened the door to exploring and executing various projects, providing a significant opportunity for growth and collaboration.

For a long time, I had dismissed the mid- to long-term business or R&D directions suggested by higher-ups as nothing more than "throwing random success stories or news articles from competitors" — leading to a sense of distrust. However, that experience became a turning point. It helped me realize that in order to drive the initiatives I was aiming for, I needed to plan projects, write reports, convince executives at partner companies, and secure funding. When working with government officials to propose and negotiate project plans, market trends — especially IoT at that time — became my powerful tool and asset.

Ⅳ. When AI Became Everyone's Language (2016–2019)

MARCH 2016
AlphaGo vs. Lee Sedol
The moment that pulled the general public into the AI market — influencing students' choice of major and career paths across the country.
JULY 4, 2019
Masayoshi Son: "First, Second, Third — All AI"
Government policy shifts, national project keyword changes, and the expansion of AI graduate schools reshaped both academic and government efforts.
July 4, 2019 — Masayoshi Son: "First, Second, and Third Are All AI" (출처: 연합뉴스)

I believe that each of us encountered AI at different times depending on our affiliations and environments. Starting with the full history of AI could lead to an endless debate, so I'd like to share my own perspective.

For me, AI became something that really pulled the general public into the market in March 2016, with the "AlphaGo vs. Lee Sedol" match, which influenced students' choice of major and career paths. In July 2019, Masayoshi Son's statement, along with government policy shifts and the expansion of AI graduate schools, played a significant role in shaping academic and government efforts around AI. At least these two moments had a major impact on me.

Ⅴ. Keywords Are the Bridge — Lessons in Communication

Looking back now, I realize that in the fast-paced decision-making processes of each organization, the key factor is how quickly you can convey your message through the approval lines.

In such cases, even seemingly obvious keywords or simple phrases become crucial — they can make a significant impact on how swiftly the message is understood and acted upon.
⚓ Captain's Take

Market trends and the keywords that represent them are not just background noise — they are the communication currency of the era. Whoever holds the right keyword at the right time holds leverage: in partnerships, in funding, in approval lines.

✅ In 2014, the keyword was IoT. In 2016, it was deep learning. In 2019, it was AI. Today, the keyword may be different — but the principle remains: reading the shift early is half the battle.
✅ Securing partnerships and investment is not purely about technology quality — it is about packaging the right message in the language decision-makers are already listening for.
✅ Two AI moments shaped my perspective: AlphaGo (March 2016) and Masayoshi Son's declaration (July 2019). These weren't just news events — they were permission slips for the broader market to take AI seriously.
✅ Mid-to-long-term guidance from senior colleagues isn't noise — it's a map. The sooner you learn to read trend signals, the more strategic your execution becomes.
